Jon A. Chun

Researcher and builder working at the frontier of AI evaluation

I combine engineering, humanistic methods and deployment experience to study how intelligent systems reason, behave and interact with people. I serve as co-PI for the Modern Language Association team in the NIST AI consortium and as co-PI and AI architect for Archival Intelligence, a Schmidt Sciences project using AI to recover endangered community archives. At Kenyon College I co-founded the AI CoLab and its human-centered AI curriculum with Katherine Elkins. Earlier I founded SafeWeb, a privacy and anti-censorship company acquired by Symantec.

Research focus

  • Reasoning

    How frontier models form judgments and reason about ethics under ambiguity, and whether their decisions hold up when the wording changes but the logic does not.

  • Behavior

    How intelligent systems act in real-world and adversarial conditions, studied through red-teaming, auditing and controlled perturbation rather than aggregate leaderboard scores.

  • Interaction

    How AI systems engage with people and institutions, including strategic interaction between agents, pragmatic understanding across social hierarchies, and governance standards that connect evidence to policy.

A current finding

The paradox of robustness

Language models are widely documented as sensitive to small prompt changes. Across controlled healthcare, law and finance scenarios we find the opposite at the decision layer: instruction-tuned models were 110 to 300 times more resistant to narrative manipulation than human subjects, with a near-zero effect size against substantial human biases. The same models remain fragile to purely syntactic reframing, which is a different and more actionable failure.

Selected recent papers

  • Syntactic Framing Fragility: An Audit of Robustness in LLM Ethical Decisions

    arXiv preprint, December 2025

    Auditing 23 state-of-the-art models over 14 ethical scenarios and four controlled framings, a total of 39,975 decisions, finds widespread inconsistency. Many models reverse an ethical endorsement on syntactic polarity alone, and open-source models show over twice the fragility of commercial ones. Chain-of-thought reasoning substantially reduces it.

  • AgenticSimLaw: A Juvenile Courtroom Multi-Agent Debate Simulation for Explainable High-Stakes Tabular Decision Making

    Accepted, AAAI-26 LaMAS, Singapore, January 2026

    Courtroom-style orchestration with explicit prosecutor, defense and judge roles, a seven-turn structured debate and private reasoning strategies. Structured multi-agent debate proves more stable and generalizable than single-agent reasoning across roughly 90 model and strategy combinations.
